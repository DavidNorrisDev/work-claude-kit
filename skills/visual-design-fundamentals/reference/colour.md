# Colour

Colour is signal, not decoration. Spend it where it carries meaning.

- Start from one primary/brand colour and build a ramp (tints for backgrounds, shades for text and states). One accent plus a neutral ramp covers most of an app.
- Balance roughly 60/30/10 (dominant neutral / secondary / accent). A bright colour over large areas dominates and usually fails contrast — lighten it and let it recede.
- **Neutral balance:** backgrounds should stay in the background. Avoid bright backgrounds; start from a neutral, optionally warmed with a hint of the brand hue. Sometimes a border beats a filled surface.
- **Semantic colour:** blue/neutral for trust, red for danger or destructive actions, amber for warning, green for success. Keep red and green available even though they aren't brand colours — a destructive action needs red regardless of brand.
- Prefer grey over pure black and white for secondary text and borders; reserve pure white/black for the few most important elements. Comfort with grey is what separates polished from flat.
- Adapt brand colours when the palette is thin or fails WCAG: rotate slightly for analogous tones, or pick a complementary colour. Accessibility wins over brand purity.
- **Check:** does every colour mean something, is the background receding, and does text pass contrast?
